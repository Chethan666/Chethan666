# Channel decisions

Decided from what makes a horror storytelling channel succeed: viewers come for a voice they trust, a recognizable world and a reliable schedule.

| Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Channel name | **Hollow Lines** | Short, unusual and memorable; no existing channel found under this name (November 2026 search). It names the story world itself ("wherever a line runs between places, there is a hollow"), so the brand and the stories reinforce each other. Tagline: *Stories from the places between.* Preferred handle `@HollowLines`, fallback `@HollowLinesStories` (check at sign-up) |
| Video format | **Still pictures, narration and music**: a new chalk picture every 3-6 seconds, each under a slow camera move, with a horror score and sound effects. No video clips | Picked when the channel was narrowed to one format (November 2026). The story and the voice carry a narration channel; pictures that change every few seconds keep the eye busy. It brings a video down to about $24 (from $30-45 with clips), and every frame is a checked chalk picture |
| Narration | **One narrator**, The Storyteller | The big storytelling channels are built on one trusted voice that viewers fall asleep to and return for. It keeps every episode consistent and avoids badly cast character voices. Character lines are read by the narrator; delivery cues (hushed, tense, tender) carry the drama |
| Narrator voice | Male, low and warm, calm, slightly weathered; unhurried (about 135 words a minute) | Matches the host on screen (a tall figure with a lantern). Calm delivery makes the scary moments land harder than a dramatic voice would |
| Storyteller ritual | Every long episode opens cold: one or two gripping sentences flashed forward from the story, then "The lantern is lit." The outro lets the fear follow the viewer home and ends with one question | Viewers decide in seconds, so the hook comes before the ritual. A ritual is a brand: loyal viewers wait for it, like a theme tune. A short line (4 words) never trips the copy checks |
| Shorts strategy | Paused for now (November 2026): long episodes only. When they return: standalone 60-second stories, plus a teaser Short cut from every long episode that stops on a cliffhanger and points to the full story | Shorts bring new viewers; teasers turn them into long-episode viewers, where the ad money is. Standalone Shorts end on a line that loops back to the first, so replays feel seamless |
| End screen | The Storyteller invites the next story in one line over the end screen | Keeps viewers on the channel, which YouTube rewards |
| Pauses | Narration notes mark up to 12 dramatic pauses (3 for Shorts) as exact phrases with lengths, made on the final script; pipeline 2 inserts the silences | Horror narration lives in its silences, and voice engines don't pause on their own |
| Rewrite or refine | Fix the deepest broken level first: a familiar premise is dropped, a weak plan is re-planned, a draft far from its plan is written again, weak passages are rewritten in place, and the best version is always kept | Live runs showed revisions that lost ground, scores stuck through rounds of prose edits, and plans that were never reviewed |
| Captions | Burned into every video, plus an uploaded subtitle file | Many viewers watch muted, especially Shorts, and non-native English speakers follow along. The subtitle file also helps search |
| Publishing time | 6 PM US Eastern (11 PM UK, 4:30 AM India) | Puts new episodes on home pages for the US evening, the largest English-speaking horror audience, and the UK late night. Revisit after 8 weeks using YouTube Analytics' "When your viewers are on YouTube" |
| Schedule | Long episodes Mon / Wed / Fri; Shorts Tue / Thu / Sat / Sun | Unchanged from the plan; regular days train viewers to come back |
| Thumbnail style | Mostly black, one white chalk figure, the red accent, at most four words | Instantly recognizable in a feed, and consistent with the pictures in the videos |
